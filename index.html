Fieldwork tools

Conversations and job shadows

Informational-interview questions
  1. What does a typical day or week actually look like?
  2. What parts of the work are most satisfying? Most difficult?
  3. Which skills matter most that outsiders may not see?
  4. What are the setting and schedule really like?
  5. How did you enter this work, and what other routes are possible?
  6. What training, licenses, or experience matter in Utah?
  7. What would you want a high-school student to understand first?
  8. What should I explore next for another perspective?

Read the preparation guide from UC Berkeley.

Job-shadow worksheet

Before: What do I want to learn? What two assumptions am I testing? What privacy, safety, dress, and phone rules apply?

During: Notice typical tasks and tools; people and collaboration; pace, setting, and movement; skills in action; surprises; and questions. Do not record private client, patient, student, or customer information.

After (500–700 words): What did I observe? Which assumptions were confirmed, challenged, or unanswered? What could I enjoy, tolerate, or avoid in this work? What is my next experiment?

Finish: Send a thank-you note within two days.
